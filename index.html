The State of the AFL

An overview of the Australian Football League - from its origins in 1897 to the present day.

Where the Game Lives

The AFL's 18 teams are spread across five Australian states, but the heartland remains Victoria, home to 10 of the league's clubs. This map shows major AFL venues and their average attendance figures.

The MCG: Australia's Sporting Cathedral

The Melbourne Cricket Ground has hosted every AFL Grand Final since 1902 and remains the largest stadium in the Southern Hemisphere, with a capacity of over 100,000.

The Fortress of the West

Perth's Optus Stadium gives West Coast and Fremantle one of the strongest home advantages in the league. The 5-hour flight from Melbourne makes it one of the toughest away trips in professional sport.

Expansion Beyond Victoria

The AFL was a Victorian-only competition until 1982. Today, venues from Alice Springs to Hobart host matches — part of the league's long-term push to make Australian football a truly national game.

A Tasmanian Future

Tasmania has hosted AFL matches for decades without a team of its own. That changes in 2028, when the state finally joins the league as the 19th club.

A History of Dominance

Since 1897, 15 teams have tasted premiership glory. Carlton, Collingwood and Essendon lead the way with 16 flags each, though the modern era has seen a more even spread of success.

How the Game Has Changed

Scoring patterns have shifted dramatically over the decades. The high-scoring era of the 1970s and 80s has given way to a more defensive, congested style of play.

The Power of Home

Home ground advantage is real in the AFL, but not all teams benefit equally. Interstate teams like West Coast and Fremantle enjoy some of the largest home-versus-away win rate gaps in the league.

The 2025 Season

Adelaide claimed the minor premiership with an 18–5 record, but it was Brisbane who went back-to-back, defeating Geelong in the Grand Final. Gold Coast made finals for the first time in the club's history.

Victoria's Grip on the Game

Despite the AFL's national expansion since 1987, Victoria still holds more than half of all teams. The distribution of venues reflects this concentration, with the MCG and Marvel Stadium hosting the bulk of matches.
