The State of the AFL

An overview of the Australian Football League, from its origins in 1897 to the present day.

Where the Game Lives

The AFL's 18 teams are spread across five Australian states, but the heartland remains Victoria, home to 10 of the league's clubs. This map shows major AFL venues and their average attendance figures.

The MCG: Australia's Sporting Cathedral

The Melbourne Cricket Ground has hosted every AFL Grand Final since 1902 and remains the largest stadium in the Southern Hemisphere, with a capacity of over 100,000.

The Fortress of the West

Perth's Optus Stadium gives West Coast and Fremantle one of the strongest home advantages in the league. The 5-hour flight from Melbourne makes it one of the toughest away trips in professional sport.

Expansion Beyond Victoria

The AFL was a Victorian-only competition until 1982. Today, venues from Alice Springs to Hobart host matches, part of the league's long-term push to make Australian football a truly national game.

A Tasmanian Future

Tasmania has hosted AFL matches for decades without a team of its own. That changes in 2028, when the state finally joins the league as the 19th club.

A History of Dominance

Since 1897, 15 teams have tasted premiership glory. Carlton, Collingwood and Essendon lead the way with 16 flags each, though the modern era has seen a more even spread of success. The game's greatest goalkickers have etched their names into history with over a thousand career goals.

How the Game Has Changed

Scoring patterns have shifted dramatically over the decades. The high-scoring era of the 1970s and 80s has given way to a more defensive, congested style of play. Meanwhile, attendance has steadily climbed as the game went national — with a sharp dip during the pandemic years.

The Power of Home

Home ground advantage is real in the AFL, but not all teams benefit equally. Interstate teams like West Coast and Fremantle enjoy some of the largest home-versus-away win rate gaps in the league.

The Perth Factor

West Coast's home advantage is the largest in the league. The combination of a hostile crowd, a 3,400 km flight, and the time zone difference makes Perth one of the hardest places for visiting teams to win.

Why Home Matters

Familiar surroundings, crowd support, and the absence of travel fatigue all contribute. Studies have shown AFL home teams win roughly 57% of matches — a figure that rises above 70% for some interstate clubs.

The Melbourne Exception

Victorian clubs share the MCG and Marvel Stadium, meaning "home" games are played at the same ground their opponents know just as well. This explains why Victorian teams tend to have smaller home advantages than interstate sides.

COVID Changed Everything

The 2020 and 2021 seasons saw teams relocated to hubs across the country. Without crowds or true home games, the traditional home advantage virtually disappeared — proving just how much the fans matter.

Victoria's Grip on the Game

Despite the AFL's national expansion since 1987, Victoria still holds more than half of all teams. The expansion timeline shows how the league grew from a Melbourne-only affair to a truly national competition over more than a century.

Essendon's Perfect Season

In 2000, Essendon went 20–2 in the home and away season, one of the most dominant campaigns in AFL history. Select 2000 to see just how far ahead they were.

Brisbane's Three-Peat

Between 2001 and 2003, the Brisbane Lions won three consecutive premierships. No team has managed a three-peat since, and it remains one of the great modern dynasties.

The Pandemic Season

The 2020 season was unlike any other. Shortened to 17 rounds, played in hubs, and held almost entirely without crowds. Richmond still found a way to claim the flag.

Gold Coast's Breakthrough

After years of wooden spoons and early exits, the Gold Coast Suns finally made the finals in 2025, a milestone moment for the league's youngest club.

The Game's Highest Honour

The Brownlow Medal is awarded annually to the best and fairest player in the AFL. Over the past two decades, the award has been shared across clubs, with the winning vote count trending upward as the game's midfield stars dominate.
